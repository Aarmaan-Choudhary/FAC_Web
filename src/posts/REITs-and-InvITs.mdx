## UNDERSTANDING REITs AND InvITs

REITs and InvITs open the door to real estate and infrastructure investing without owning physical assets. Built on regulated, exchange-listed frameworks, their core appeal lies in **mandated cash distributions**, which makes them income-focused instruments rather than aggressive growth bets.

---

### WHAT ARE THEY?

- **SEBI-regulated investment trusts**, listed on Indian stock exchanges since **2014**.
- They own **income-generating assets**:
  - **REITs** (Real Estate Investment Trusts): offices, shops and malls.
  - **InvITs** (Infrastructure Investment Trusts): roads, power plants and transmission lines.
- They must pay out most of their cash to investors: **at least 90% of net distributable cash**.
- **Why they exist**: to give investors predictable income.

---

### HOW INVESTORS GET PAID

1. The underlying assets earn cash: rent, tolls, power sales and so on.
2. Expenses, debt and maintenance are paid first. What remains is the **Net Distributable Cash Flow (NDCF)**.
3. This cash reaches investors in three forms:
   - **Interest**
   - **Dividends**
   - **Capital repayment**

### WHY SHOULD INVESTORS CONSIDER THEM?

- **Stocks** pay unpredictable dividends; **REITs** make regular cash payouts.
- **Required distributions** mean managers can't hoard cash.
- They are **less affected by business cycles** than ordinary companies.
- They **diversify a portfolio**, being less tied to the stock market's ups and downs.

---

### THE BOTTOM LINE

REITs and InvITs suit investors who want steady, predictable income and exposure to real estate or infrastructure without buying property. Their payouts are attractive, but growth is limited, and returns depend on interest rates, the economy and government policy. Weigh these risks before allocating capital.
